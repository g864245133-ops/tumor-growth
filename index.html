7Methods & rationale

Why these methods? (statistics and unequal starts explained)

Tumor volume

Volume is calculated with the modified ellipsoid formula V = L × W² / 2 (Euler et al.; widely used for subcutaneous xenografts), where L is the longest diameter and W the perpendicular shorter diameter. π/6 × L × W² (≈ 0.52 × L × W²) is offered as an alternative. If W > L in a row, the two are swapped automatically.

Whole-curve comparison (growth trend)

Primary test: permutation test of growth curves (Elso et al., 2004, Genes & Immunity; Baldwin et al., 2007; implemented in R as statmod::compareGrowthCurves). For each pair of groups, a t-statistic is computed at every time point and averaged over time. Group labels are then randomly re-assigned among the animals (whole curves are permuted, so the repeated-measures structure of each animal is preserved) thousands of times. The p-value is the proportion of permuted statistics at least as extreme as the observed one. With 3 or more groups, an overall test uses the time-averaged one-way ANOVA F statistic in the same way. Pairwise p-values are Holm-adjusted. Why this method: it makes no assumption about the shape of the curve or about normality, it handles animals that leave the study early (time points with fewer than 2 animals per group are skipped), and it is valid for the small group sizes typical of mouse studies.

Supporting analyses: per-animal summary measures (Matthews et al., 1990, BMJ). Each animal's curve is reduced to a single number, so each animal contributes exactly one value and standard two-group or multi-group tests apply. Specific growth rate is the slope of ln(V+1) against time (log-linear regression). It is independent of the starting volume, and the doubling time is ln2 / rate. Area under the curve (AUC) uses the trapezoidal rule up to the last day on which all animals are still measured, so that animals removed early do not bias it.

Comparison on each day

Two groups: Welch's t-test (does not assume equal variances). Three or more groups: Welch's one-way ANOVA, followed by pairwise Welch t-tests with Holm correction. Non-parametric option: Mann-Whitney U (exact when there are no ties) and Kruskal-Wallis. Tumor volumes are right-skewed and their variance grows with the mean, so volumes are log-transformed, as ln(V+1), before testing by default. Testing each day separately inflates false positives, so the Holm correction can be applied across all days. The whole-curve test (A) is the preferred single answer to "do the groups grow differently?".

When start dates differ

Animals are aligned by day from treatment start (or day from injection), not by calendar date. Each animal's day 0 is its own start, so cohorts enrolled on different dates are directly comparable. If only dates are given for some rows, the missing day numbers are filled in from that animal's other rows (date − day = its start date). Without day columns, days are counted from each animal's first measurement.

If animals were measured on different days (e.g. day 7 vs. day 8), the "Interpolate to a common day grid" option estimates each animal's volume on a shared grid. Interpolation is linear on the log scale (exponential growth between measurements) and only within each animal's observed range; it never extrapolates.

When baseline volumes differ

1) The baseline check (section 3) tests whether groups differ at the start. 2) Relative tumor volume (V / V₀) or % change normalizes each animal to its own baseline. 3) ANCOVA, with ln(V+1) adjusted for ln(V₀+1) as a covariate, is the most efficient adjustment when baselines differ. It is recommended over simple normalization when the imbalance is large (Vickers & Altman, 2001, BMJ). 4) The growth rate (slope on the log scale) is unaffected by the starting size. Best practice is to randomize animals to groups once tumors reach a target size, so baselines are balanced from the start.
